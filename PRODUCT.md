# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two friends playing from separate locations, usually on phones or laptops, who want a fast private UNO match without creating an account. The primary job is to create or join a room, understand the live turn state, and make legal moves with confidence.

## Product Purpose

UNO DUEL is a realtime two-player UNO table. It creates a private room, keeps the authoritative match on the server, protects each player's hand, and makes reconnecting after a refresh feel safe. Success is a complete round played by two remote players, followed by a clear rematch path.

## Positioning

The quickest way for two friends to sit down at the same UNO table, even when they are not in the same room.

## Brand Personality

Loud, playful, and trustworthy. The interface should feel like a late-night tabletop with bright card color, clear state, and a little competitive theater. Celebration can be expressive; errors and connection problems stay direct and calm.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, fake matchmaking or player counts, pass-the-device interactions, excessive glass panels, decorative gradients, tiny clickable cards, and any visual treatment that reveals hidden opponent cards.

## Design Principles

- Put the match first: the turn, legal move, and connection state should be understood at a glance.
- Make remote play feel present: show names, presence, last action, and hand counts without exposing private cards.
- Use spectacle for accepted moments only: the table can celebrate a real play, UNO call, or win, never pretend a command succeeded.
- Keep the rules honest: every browser action is a request to the server, and missing infrastructure is visible.
- Make the small screen a real table: keep the piles and active hand usable without requiring drag gestures.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic controls, visible focus states, text labels alongside color, descriptive card labels, polite live announcements, keyboard-operable hands, mobile-safe touch targets, and reduced-motion alternatives. Do not rely on color alone for turn, presence, or card identity.
